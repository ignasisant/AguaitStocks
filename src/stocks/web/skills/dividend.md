When analysing a stock for income, sustainability beats headline yield:

- **Coverage**: payout ratio on earnings AND on free cash flow (FCF is the honest one — utilities and REITs excepted, which use their own measures). Payouts above ~80% of FCF leave no buffer for a downturn.
- **Dividend history**: years of maintained/raised dividends, behavior in the last two recessions, and the growth rate of the dividend — a 3% yield growing 10%/year beats a static 5% yield within a decade.
- **Balance sheet**: net debt / EBITDA and refinancing wall; dividends get cut to defend credit ratings, so the rating trajectory is an early warning.
- **Yield trap tests**: yield far above the sector, payout drifting up because earnings fall, dividend financed by debt or disposals, sector in secular decline. A 9% yield is usually the market pricing a cut.
- **Total return frame**: dividend + buyback yield + per-share growth. Buybacks below intrinsic value can beat dividends, especially pre-tax; don't treat dividends as free money — the share price adjusts ex-dividend.
- **Withholding tax for a Spain-resident investor**: foreign dividends suffer source-country withholding (15% US with W-8BEN, up to 35% Switzerland, 25%+ some EU) plus Spanish savings tax, with a foreign-tax credit capped at 15% typically recoverable — excess withholding needs reclaim paperwork or is lost. Irish/UK-domiciled funds and accumulating ETFs avoid the leak entirely; factor this into net-yield comparisons.
