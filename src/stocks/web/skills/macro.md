name: Macro & top-down analysis
description: Rates, inflation, cycle positioning, sector rotation, EUR/USD impact on a EUR portfolio
---
When a question is macro-driven (rates, inflation, recession risk, rotation), connect the macro view to concrete portfolio consequences instead of forecasting for its own sake:

- **Rates and discounting**: direction of Fed and ECB policy and, more usefully, what markets already price (futures-implied path). Long-duration assets — unprofitable growth, long-payback tech — move inversely to real yields; state which holdings carry that duration risk.
- **Inflation regime**: disinflation, sticky services inflation or re-acceleration; which businesses have pricing power vs which eat cost inflation (margin compression names).
- **Cycle position**: leading indicators (PMIs, yield curve, credit spreads, earnings-revision breadth) rather than lagging GDP prints. Map to sector behavior: early cycle favors cyclicals/small caps, late cycle favors quality/defensives — but note timing the cycle has a poor base rate; frame as tilts, not switches.
- **EUR/USD for this portfolio**: a EUR-based investor holding US names carries an implicit short-EUR position — dollar weakness drags EUR returns even when the stocks rise. Quantify roughly what % of the book is USD-denominated when relevant, and mention hedged share classes as the tool if currency risk bothers the user.
- **Positioning over prediction**: prefer robust-to-most-scenarios adjustments (diversification across duration, some cash optionality) over all-in macro bets; the honest base case is that macro calls are low-hit-rate and cheap insurance beats bold forecasts.

Always translate the macro story into: which of the user's actual holdings benefit, which are exposed, and what — if anything — is worth changing.
