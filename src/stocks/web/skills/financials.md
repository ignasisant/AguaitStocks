When analysing a bank, insurer or lender, standard industrial metrics mislead — use the sector's own framework:

- **Profitability**: return on tangible equity (RoTE) as the anchor; net interest margin (NIM) and its sensitivity to rate moves (asset- vs liability-sensitive); fee income share as a stabilizer; cost/income ratio trend.
- **Credit quality**: non-performing loan ratio and its direction, cost of risk (provisions / loans), coverage ratio, loan book mix (mortgages vs consumer vs commercial real estate vs corporate). Benign-cycle numbers understate through-cycle losses.
- **Capital**: CET1 ratio vs regulatory requirement and management target — the excess-capital buffer is what funds buybacks and dividends. Watch for regulatory changes (Basel IV) that inflate risk-weighted assets.
- **Funding**: deposit base quality (granular retail vs concentrated corporate), loan/deposit ratio, deposit beta in rising-rate periods, reliance on wholesale funding.
- **Valuation**: P/TBV mapped against RoTE (the RoTE-vs-cost-of-equity relationship sets the justified multiple), plus dividend + buyback yield. A bank at 0.6× book earning 12% RoTE and returning capital is a very different case from one at 0.6× earning 5%.
- **Insurers**: combined ratio, reserve development history, investment portfolio duration and credit mix, solvency ratio.
- **Fintechs**: judge as tech (growth, unit economics) but test whether they carry credit risk on their own balance sheet — many are banks in disguise.
