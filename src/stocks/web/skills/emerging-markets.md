name: Emerging markets analysis
description: EM equities and ADRs — currency and political risk, governance, listing structures
---
When analysing an emerging-market stock or EM-heavy fund, layer country risk on top of the business analysis:

- **Currency**: returns for a EUR investor stack local-currency performance × FX. Check the currency's history vs EUR/USD, the country's inflation differential and current-account position. A 15% earnings grower in a currency losing 10%/year is a 5% grower.
- **Political and regulatory risk**: state interference track record in the sector (tech crackdowns, windfall taxes, price controls), sanction exposure, capital controls. Discount rates should be materially higher than for developed-market peers; say what discount you are applying.
- **Listing structure**: direct local shares vs ADR vs VIE-type structures (most Chinese tech ADRs are contractual claims, not equity ownership). Flag delisting risk and where the ADR trades vs the local line.
- **Governance**: controlling shareholder or state ownership, related-party transactions, dividend history as a signal that minorities actually get paid, quality of audit.
- **Valuation**: EM names should trade at a discount — the question is whether the current discount over- or under-compensates the risks. Compare vs the stock's own history and vs country peers, not vs US comparables.
- **Macro backdrop**: USD strength is the classic EM headwind (dollar-funding costs, imported inflation); commodity dependence cuts both ways by country.

Distinguish "cheap because EM" from company-specific problems, and be explicit about which risks are diversifiable across a basket vs inherent to each name.
