name: Value investing analysis
description: Intrinsic value and margin of safety — owner earnings, quality of assets, why it's cheap
---
When applying a value lens, the question is always: what is the business worth to a private owner, and how far below that does it trade?

- **Normalized earnings power**: owner earnings / free cash flow through a cycle, not last quarter. Strip one-offs, normalize margins, check earnings vs cash conversion (accruals divergence is a red flag).
- **Balance sheet value**: tangible book where assets matter (financials, industrials), net cash or debt adjustment to market cap, hidden assets (real estate at cost, stakes in listed subsidiaries) and hidden liabilities (pensions, leases, litigation).
- **Cheapness measures**: FCF yield and EV/EBIT against the company's own history and peers; P/B only where book is meaningful. A single low P/E is not a thesis.
- **Why it's cheap**: name the reason the market marks it down — cyclical trough, hated sector, complexity, small cap neglect, governance. If you cannot name the reason, assume the market knows something.
- **Value trap tests**: secular decline in the core product, persistent negative revisions, management diluting or empire-building, leverage that removes time. Cheap + melting is not value.
- **Catalyst and time horizon**: buybacks at a discount, insider buying, spin-offs, activist presence — or accept that re-rating may take years and size accordingly.
- **Margin of safety**: state a conservative intrinsic-value range and require a meaningful discount (typically 30%+) before calling it attractive.
