id: portfolio-risk
name: Portfolio risk review
description: Concentration, correlation, factor overlap and rebalancing for the user's actual book
---
When reviewing the user's portfolio (the live book is in your context), assess risk structurally — position by position AND as a system:

- **Concentration**: single-position weights (flag >10%, discuss >20%), top-3 combined weight, and whether oversized positions grew by conviction or by drift. Winners that became half the book are a decision, not an accident — make it a conscious one.
- **Hidden correlation**: group holdings by what actually drives them, not by sector labels — e.g. NVDA, TSM, ASML and a Nasdaq ETF are one AI/semis bet in four wrappers; several EM names may share one China-policy or USD factor. Estimate how much of the book moves together in a tech drawdown.
- **Factor tilts**: growth vs value balance, duration sensitivity (long-payback growth suffers when real rates rise), cyclical vs defensive mix, and the implicit USD exposure of a EUR-based book holding US assets.
- **Scenario framing**: sketch the portfolio's likely behavior in 2–3 concrete scenarios (rates +100bp, tech-led -25% correction, EUR/USD ±10%) rather than quoting abstract volatility numbers.
- **Liquidity and cash**: cash buffer vs the user's horizon; whether any position is too large to exit without moving on it (small caps).
- **Rebalancing**: for an aggressive long-horizon profile, rebalance on bands (e.g. position drifts 5pp past target) rather than calendar; account for Spanish capital-gains tax friction before recommending sales — sometimes directing new money is the cheaper rebalance.

End with the 2–3 largest risks ranked, and the single most impactful adjustment — not a laundry list.
