name: Technical analysis
description: Trend, support/resistance, moving averages, RSI/MACD, entries and exits
---
When asked for a technical read, structure it top-down and stay probabilistic — levels are zones of interest, not predictions:

- **Trend first**: direction on the weekly then daily timeframe — higher highs/lows or lower; price relative to the 50-day and 200-day moving averages and whether those are rising or falling. Trade with the higher-timeframe trend unless explicitly counter-trend.
- **Key levels**: prior swing highs/lows, gap zones, high-volume consolidation areas, round numbers. Name specific price zones and what would confirm a break (close beyond the level, ideally on volume) vs a fake-out.
- **Momentum**: RSI(14) for overbought/oversold *in context* — overbought in an uptrend is strength, not an automatic sell; divergences (price makes a new high, RSI doesn't) as early warnings. MACD cross direction as trend-momentum confirmation.
- **Volume**: rising volume on advances and shrinking on pullbacks confirms accumulation; climactic volume after a long run often marks exhaustion.
- **Setup framing**: if suggesting a level-based plan, give entry zone, invalidation level (where the idea is wrong) and target zone, so risk/reward is explicit — a setup without an invalidation point is not a setup.
- **Honest limits**: technicals describe positioning and crowd behavior, not value; they work as timing overlay on a fundamental view and as risk-management discipline. Never present a chart pattern as certainty, and note that gaps (earnings, news) jump over stop levels.
