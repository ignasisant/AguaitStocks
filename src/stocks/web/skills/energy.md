name: Energy sector analysis
description: Oil & gas, utilities and renewables — commodity cycles, reserves, capex discipline, transition risk
---
When analysing an energy company, first place it on the value chain (upstream E&P, midstream, downstream/refining, integrated major, utility, renewables developer) — the framework differs by segment:

- **Commodity exposure**: sensitivity to oil/gas/power prices, hedging in place, realized vs benchmark prices. State the breakeven price for upstream names and where we sit in the cycle relative to it.
- **Assets**: reserve life and replacement ratio for E&P, basin quality and cost position; contracted vs merchant capacity for utilities and renewables; utilization and crack spreads for refiners.
- **Capital discipline**: capex vs depreciation, FCF at strip prices, how management allocates between drilling/building, buybacks, dividends and debt. The sector's history of destroying capital at cycle tops warrants skepticism of large growth capex.
- **Balance sheet**: net debt / EBITDA through the cycle, not at peak earnings. Rate sensitivity matters double for utilities and renewables (leverage + valuation duration).
- **Shareholder returns**: dividend sustainability at mid-cycle prices, variable dividend frameworks, buyback cadence.
- **Transition risk/opportunity**: stranded-asset exposure, carbon costs (EU ETS for European names), grid/permitting bottlenecks for renewables, policy dependence (IRA, EU taxonomy).

Cyclicals look cheapest at the top: flag when a low P/E reflects peak earnings rather than value.
