name: ETF and fund analysis
description: ETFs, index and traded funds — cost, tracking, overlap with the book, accumulating vs distributing, UCITS/Spain angle
---
A fund is a wrapper, so the analysis is about the wrapper and the basket, never about "the company". Fundamental language (moat, margins, DCF) does not apply — refuse to fake it and use these lenses instead:

- **What it actually tracks**: the index and its rules (market-cap weighted, equal weight, screened, factor tilt), and whether the fund replicates physically or synthetically (swap-based). Say which, because it decides the risks below.
- **Cost, all of it**: TER is the visible part; add tracking difference vs the index over 1–3 years, bid-ask spread at the size traded, and turnover. A cheap TER with wide spreads is not cheap for someone buying monthly.
- **Overlap with what's already held**: the single biggest mistake in a book that mixes stocks and ETFs. A world ETF plus a Nasdaq ETF plus four US tech names is one concentrated bet — quantify the double count through the fund's sector and top-holding weights rather than counting positions.
- **Concentration inside the basket**: number of holdings, top-10 weight, and single-name cap. "Diversified" is a claim to check, not a property of being an ETF: some sector and thematic funds run 40%+ in ten names.
- **Accumulating (Acc) vs distributing (Dist)**: for a Spanish resident this is a fiscal choice as much as a preference — Acc defers the taxable event and compounds gross, Dist pays income that is taxed as savings income in the year received. Say which share class the ticker is when it is knowable from its name.
- **UCITS vs US-listed**: EU retail investors generally cannot buy US-domiciled ETFs (no KID), and an Irish-domiciled UCITS fund usually recovers more US dividend withholding than a Luxembourg one. Domicile also decides estate-tax exposure on US-situs assets.
- **Currency exposure vs currency hedging**: the fund's trading currency is not its exposure — a EUR-listed world ETF is mostly USD risk. Only a share class explicitly labelled hedged removes it, and hedging costs the rate differential.
- **Liquidity and size**: fund AUM, average volume and whether a small fund risks closure or merger (a forced realisation of gains at the wrong moment).

For bond funds, replace the sector lens with duration, credit quality and yield-to-maturity, and be explicit that price moves inversely to rates by roughly duration × the rate move.

Never present index or fund past returns as expected returns; frame ranges and say what would have to hold for them.
